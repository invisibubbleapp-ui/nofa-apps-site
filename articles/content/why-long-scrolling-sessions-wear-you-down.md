# Why Long Scrolling Sessions Wear You Down (Short Version)

*General information, not medical advice. For problems with sleep, mood or daily life, talk to a doctor or a mental-health professional.*

Scrolling for hours is rarely a decision. The feed has no end, so stopping takes a choice and continuing takes none. Here is what research says, in short.

**Sleep.** A 2024 meta-analysis found that problematic social media use, meaning use that feels hard to control, is associated with sleep problems, though not with how long people sleep. Social media use on its own was not.

**Mood and anxiety.** The same review found small but significant associations between social media use and both depression and anxiety. Studies on doomscrolling link it with more anxiety and stress. Most are surveys, so they show links, not cause.

**Eyes.** The American Academy of Ophthalmology describes digital eye strain: blurry vision, headaches, tired and dry eyes, partly because we blink less on screens.

**Neck and body.** "Text neck" evidence is weaker than the headlines. Neck pain is real and common, but the research is mixed. Long sitting matters too: the WHO links higher sedentary time with poorer health outcomes.

**Is it a disorder?** Gaming disorder is in ICD-11. Social media addiction is not currently an independent diagnosis, and doomscrolling is a name for a behaviour, not a diagnosis.

**What helps.** Decide your limit before you open the app. Make the stop visible. Use a tool if you like, but expect modest results: a meta-analysis of digital self-control tools found a small-to-medium effect on reducing time, mostly short term. Protect your sleep, move, and rest your eyes.
