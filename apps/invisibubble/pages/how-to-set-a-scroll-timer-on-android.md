To set a scroll timer on Android, install {{app.name}} from Google Play, allow it to display over other apps, then tap the bubble and choose "Scroll timer" before you open Reels or Shorts. When the time is up, a full-screen takeover asks if you are done or want extra time.

The steps below take a few minutes the first time. After that, you only need step 4 each time you scroll.

## Step 1: Install Invisibubble

Open the [Invisibubble page on Google Play]({{app.playUrl}}) and install the app. It works on Android phones only.

## Step 2: Allow "Display over other apps"

The bubble and the time's up screen show on top of other apps. Android needs your permission for this. When the app asks, turn on **Display over other apps**.

The name of this setting can differ by phone maker. On some phones it is called "Appear on top" or "Draw over other apps". The [permissions page]({{url:is-it-safe-and-what-permissions}}) explains what it does.

## Step 3: Check that the bubble is at the edge

You should now see a small glass bubble at the edge of your screen. It stays there while you use other apps.

## Step 4: Set your minutes before you scroll

Before you open a scrolling app, set the timer. {{app.openTimer}} Then choose 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes, or set a custom time.

## Step 5: Open Reels or Shorts and scroll

Open Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts or any other app. The timer keeps counting while the bubble stays at the edge. {{app.timerRuns}}

## Step 6: Choose what to do when time is up

When the time ends, a full-screen takeover appears. Tap **"I'm done"** if you are finished, or add extra time. The second time you add extra time, you need to type a reason first.

## Tips for limiting time on Reels and Shorts

- **Set the timer before you open the app.** It is easier to choose a limit before the feed starts. Our article on [what hours of scrolling can do to your sleep, mood, focus and body]({{article:what-scrolling-does-to-sleep-mood-focus-and-body}}) explains why deciding first helps.
- **Start small.** You can always add time. The second extension asks for a reason, which gives you a moment to decide.
- **Use reminders.** {{app.reminders}}
- **Hide the bubble when you need to.** The "Hide bubble" option hides it for a time you choose.

Android also has its own daily app timers on many phones. The [comparison with app blockers and screen time limits]({{url:vs-app-blockers}}) explains how they differ.

## FAQ

### Does Android have a built-in scroll timer?

Many Android phones have Digital Wellbeing, which lets you set a daily timer for each app. Invisibubble works differently: you set a timer yourself each time you start scrolling.

### Which timer lengths can I choose?

You can choose 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes, or set a custom time.

### What happens if I want more time?

You can add extra time on the time's up screen. The second extension asks you to type a reason first.

### Does this work for YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels?

Yes. Invisibubble shows on top of other apps, so the timer runs while you use YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and other apps. It is not affiliated with YouTube or Instagram.
